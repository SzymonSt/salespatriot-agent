# Capability verification — how governments check suppliers today

Question from the team: how do governments check that a supplier really has the machines,
people and capacity, and how long does it take? Answer below, with no invented numbers.

## Two different things
1. **Supplier qualification** — is the firm formally and legally able to take part?
2. **Capability verification** — does it really have the resources, technology, staff and
   capacity to deliver *this* order?

A firm can hold every certificate and still be unable to make 10,000 parts by the deadline.
There is **no single European due-diligence process**: it varies by country, contract type,
value, equipment category and security level.

## Typical stages (our synthesis, not a mandatory sequence)
1. **Initial screening** — registration, legal status, finances, past contracts, exclusion grounds.
2. **Technical capability** — machines, technology, staff skills, production experience.
3. **Quality assurance** — QMS, ISO 9001 / EN 9100, QC procedures.
4. **Security of supply** — origin of key parts, subcontractor locations, foreign
   dependencies, surge capacity.
5. **On-site audit** (risk-based) — plant visit, staff interviews, process checks, sampling.
6. **Final qualification** — admitted to the next stage or not.

Not every supplier gets a full factory audit.

## EU law — Directive 2009/81/EC (defence and security procurement) `[REPORTED + OWN KNOWLEDGE — re-read EUR-Lex before quoting article numbers]`
| Article | Scope |
|---|---|
| 39 | Personal situation of the candidate (exclusion grounds) |
| 40 | Suitability to pursue the professional activity |
| 41 | Economic and financial standing |
| 42 | Technical and/or professional ability |
| 43 | Quality assurance standards |
| 44 | Environmental management standards |
| 45 | Additional documentation and information |
| 46 | Official lists of approved economic operators; certification |

Article 42 lets the buyer ask for, among others: deliveries/works from the last 5 years;
technicians and technical bodies; description of technical facilities; quality-control
measures; research facilities; qualifications of managers and staff; average headcount over
3 years; tools, equipment, know-how and supply sources. It also allows **checks of the
supplier's production and research capacity by the authorities**, in some cases by a
competent body in the supplier's own country.

Limits: requirements must relate to and be proportionate to the contract. The directive does
not force a physical audit of every supplier.

**Big caveat** `[OWN KNOWLEDGE]`: much defence buying is exempt from the directive under
**Art. 346 TFEU** (essential security interests) and national exemptions (in Polish law,
e.g. Art. 12–13 Pzp). Those purchases may never appear on TED.

## United Kingdom — the most concrete public guidance
- MOD DSPCR Chapter 13 (Security of Supply) lets buyers ask for: tools and technical
  equipment, materials, headcount, know-how, supply sources, location of foreign suppliers —
  and the ability to meet **extra demand in a crisis** (e.g. surge from 1,000 to 2,000 units;
  illustration, not a UK rule). `[REPORTED]`
- It also allows asking for supply-chain documentation, key components, single points of
  failure, spread of production sites, alternative production/transport options, and a
  commitment to report supply-chain changes. `[REPORTED]`
- Restricted-procedure **minimum** periods (historic MOD summary): contract notice 30 days ·
  PQQ response 30 days · supplier evaluation (no minimum) · ITT 40 days · standstill 10 days
  → **≥110 days of fixed periods**, before evaluation, negotiation etc. This is the whole
  competitive phase, not due diligence alone. `[REPORTED]`
- These DSPCR chapters are historic: the UK **Procurement Act 2023 took effect on
  24 Feb 2025** and replaced them for new procurements. `[OWN KNOWLEDGE]`
- No public statistic found on what share of UK defence suppliers get factory visits.

## NATO — AQAP quality requirements
- AQAP 2110 (design, development, production), AQAP 2310 (aviation, space, defence
  suppliers), AQAP 2210 (software add-on). Chosen per product, risk and complexity.
- **AQAP is not a certificate.** NATO does not issue AQAP certificates to companies.
  AQAP is applied as a contractual requirement.
- The Bundeswehr notes that ISO 9001 / EN 9100 raise confidence in the QMS but do **not**
  prove the product meets the spec.
- Government Quality Assurance (GQA) is done by national authorities, also on behalf of
  other nations (STANAG 4107 / AQAP-2070). In Poland this is done at contractors' plants by
  the Regional Military Representative Offices (*Rejonowe Przedstawicielstwa Wojskowe*)
  linked to the Armament Agency. `[OWN KNOWLEDGE — verify]`

## NSPA (NATO Support and Procurement Agency) `[VERIFIED 2026-10-30]`
- Suppliers must be registered in the **NSPA Source File** to receive solicitations
  (OI 4200-02, Mar 2024). Firms without matching registered capabilities are not invited.
- Eligibility factors: legal residency, national eligibility status, present capability,
  financial standing, past performance. Final decision rests with NSPA.
- Registration needs an active **NCAGE code**; some nations add a national MoD confirmation
  step (e.g. Czechia). Canada reports ~2 weeks, one-time. Free for firms from NSPO nations.

## Poland
- **Agencja Uzbrojenia** (Armament Agency): runs acquisition procedures, supervises the
  quality of delivered products, prepares and supervises offset agreements, manages IP rights.
- **No public, detailed AU due-diligence procedure found**: number of plant visits,
  documents checked per stage, how real capacity is measured, staff hours per supplier,
  average qualification time — all unknown. Do not attribute a process to AU.
- **New contractor certification** `[VERIFIED 2026-10-30]`: Act of 5 Aug 2025; voluntary;
  assesses experience, scope of past deliveries, technologies, staff qualifications,
  technical and professional ability; PCA accepts accreditation applications for certifying
  bodies from 14 Jul 2026; certificates go into a free public database. It is **not** a
  defence-specific production-capacity certification.

## Existing assessment schemes
| Scheme | What it checks | Method |
|---|---|---|
| DEKRA CADIS | Cyber/info security | LoE 1 self-assessment + plausibility · LoE 2 docs + remote interview · LoE 3 docs + on-site audit |
| NATO AQAP | QA requirements in contracts | Contract clauses + GQA oversight |
| PL contractor certification | Experience, technical/professional ability | Document-based certification |
| NSPA Source File | Eligibility + declared capabilities | Registration + national confirmation |

None of them automatically verifies a European firm's real production capacity. **That gap
is our opportunity.**

## What this means for us → three evidence levels
| Level | Name | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Declared** | Supplier's own profile ("15 CNC machines, ISO 9001, 50 staff") |
| 2 | **Document-verified** | Certificates, machine lists, references, past contracts, registry data |
| 3 | **Independently verified** | Audit, plant inspection, production test, accredited body |

The buyer sees not only a match score but **how strong the evidence is**.

| Task | AI potential |
|---|---|
| Reading certificates | Very high |
| Extracting machine lists from documents | Very high |
| Checking past contracts (TED awards) | High |
| Comparing tender requirements with capabilities | Very high |
| Spotting inconsistencies across documents | High |
| Estimating production capacity | Medium — needs fresh data |
| Confirming the real number of available machines | Needs independent proof |
| Physical factory audit | Cannot be replaced by an LLM |

## The biggest gap: current, real capacity
Even if we know a firm has 20 CNC machines, we don't know: how many are running, free
shifts, % already booked, material suitability, staff availability, raw-material stock,
ability to scale in 30 days. → Supplier agent updates capacity periodically; every capacity
claim carries `observed_at` and an evidence level.

## Positioning
Not "we replace government due diligence". Instead: **pre-qualification intelligence** — a
structured, sourced supplier profile ready *before* the formal procedure starts.
Buyers spend less time on first-pass screening; suppliers prepare once and reuse; the
marketplace compares firms on one capability model. Product: a **living supplier profile**
that updates with new certificates, contracts, documents and capacity data.

Standard formats worth reusing `[OWN KNOWLEDGE]`: **ESPD** (European Single Procurement
Document; Polish *JEDZ*) for self-declarations, and **eCertis** (EU tool mapping which
certificates prove what in each member state).
