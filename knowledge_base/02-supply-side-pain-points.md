# Supply-side pain points (suppliers, SMEs, Tier 2–4)

The European defence industry is not just Rheinmetall, Leonardo, Thales or Airbus. It is
thousands of firms making components, materials, electronics, optics, software and
mechanical parts, plus industrial services. This is where the biggest opportunity sits.

## 1. Supplier discovery — firms can't reach the right customers
Example (illustrative): a Polish firm with 5-axis CNC machines, ISO 9001, 20% idle capacity,
no defence track record. It could be a good subcontractor for a European systems maker, but
it does not know:
- which prime needs such parts;
- which tenders match its skills;
- how to get onto a prime's supplier list;
- which certificates are required;
- whether it can join as a subcontractor instead of bidding for the whole tender.

**Pain:** the firm has capability but no market intelligence and no access to buying networks.
**Agent opportunity:** supplier agent that watches tenders and primes' needs, and proposes
"enter as subcontractor to X" instead of "bid for everything".

## 2. Demand uncertainty — firms don't know if investment pays off
Many European defence firms build to order. They need predictable orders, long-term
contracts, financing for machines, staff and raw materials before adding capacity.

Vicious circle: uncertain government demand → supplier avoids CAPEX → capacity stays
limited → buyers face delays and shortages → repeat. `[REPORTED]` (EPRS 2025)

Norway's government roadmap names long-term binding contracts with industry as the
central tool to break this circle. `[VERIFIED 2026-10-30]`

**Agent opportunity:** spot future demand signals and recurring purchases, not only open tenders.

## 3. Tier 2 / Tier 3 visibility — nobody sees the whole chain
Ministry → Prime → Tier 1 → Tier 2 (electronics, mechanics, materials) → Tier 3.
A prime knows its direct suppliers, often not the lower tiers.

US evidence: DoD estimates over 200,000 suppliers help produce weapon systems and other
goods; visibility efforts are disjointed; an Air Force review of three commercial
supply-chain mapping tools found them 60–70% accurate, flagging risks that did not exist and
missing some suppliers. `[VERIFIED 2026-10-30]` (GAO-25-107283)

**Pain:** no reliable map of who depends on whom.
**Agent opportunity:** supplier knowledge graph built from tenders, award notices, company
sites and registries — but it must separate confirmed links from guessed ones. The GAO
finding shows auto-built graphs alone are not enough.

## 4. Compliance and certification — entry is expensive
Being able to make the part is not enough. Typical requirements:
- ISO 9001 (quality management);
- EN 9100 / AS9100 (aerospace and defence quality);
- AQAP 2110 / 2310 / 2210 (NATO contractual quality requirements — see [05](05-capability-verification.md));
- information security, and for classified work an industrial security clearance
  (in Poland: *świadectwo bezpieczeństwa przemysłowego*) `[OWN KNOWLEDGE]`;
- origin-of-components rules, export licences (EU dual-use regulation 2021/821) `[OWN KNOWLEDGE]`;
- for NATO/NSPA business: an NCAGE code and NSPA Source File registration `[VERIFIED 2026-10-30]`.

For a small firm, even working out which rules apply to one tender is hard.

**Agent opportunity:** *supplier readiness assessment* per tender — what the firm already
meets, what is missing, which documents are needed, and which partner could fill the gap.

## 5. Cash flow and production financing
A firm can win a contract and still lack money to deliver it: raw materials up front,
machines, long production cycles, certification and audit costs, late payments, unclear
follow-on orders.

- ~40% of defence SMEs report difficult access to finance (DG DEFIS study, Jan 2024; repeated
  in 2026 coverage of the European Parliament SEDE study). `[REPORTED]`
- EU Payment Observatory: average payment terms above 60 days in B2B and G2B; over half of
  firms report late-payment problems. `[REPORTED]`

Not defence-specific, but harder on small firms with long production cycles.
**Agent opportunity (later):** flag payment terms and advance-payment clauses in tenders;
point to funding instruments (EDF, SAFE, national programmes).

## Summary for agents
| Pain | Data we can use today | Data we must collect from suppliers |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | TED notices, award winners, CPV | Capabilities in the firm's own words |
| Demand uncertainty | Historical award frequency per buyer/CPV | — |
| Tier 2/3 visibility | Award notices (winners, sometimes subcontracting info) | Actual sub-supplier links |
| Compliance | Tender requirements text | Certificates (upload + check) |
| Cash flow | Payment terms in tender docs | Financing needs |
